## 0.40.0 (2026-11-21)

The crust under the land, its unroofing, what it sheds and where the sediment rests (owner's direction of 20 November 2026, the causal-chain audit, items 3 to 5, as an account on the map's squares; the owner chose this bookkeeping layer first, so that Resources has what it needs, and the land's shapes are not changed by it). A new file, `data/eym/crust.json`, made by `tools/generate-eym-crust.mjs` in the build chain (dials `sediment`).

- *The crust that floats each land square* (Airy isostasy): 35 km under land at the sea's level to 70 km under the highest squares, 40 km in the mean. Under northern Caren the mantle's swell holds part of the height, so its crust is thinner than the height alone would ask (as its own survey says).
- *How deep each square is unroofed:* a range's own unroofing near the range (0.39.0); at least 10 km wherever rock that formed deep (granite, gneiss, metamorphic rock, marble, quartzite) is at the surface; nothing under a cover of sediment or lava. 7,211 land squares have deep rock at the surface.
- *How fast each square wears down,* from how far it stands above its lowest neighbour, the water it sheds (the rain budget's, less what soil and plant take) and its rock's hardness (granite and quartzite slow, shale fast): 3 to 1,364 m a million years, 13 in the middle square and 45 in the mean (Earth's mean is about 50).
- *Where the sediment rests* (`scripts/eym-sediment.mjs`). What a square sheds goes downhill square by square to the sea (the land is flooded from the sea upward, so every square has a way out unless it lies in a hollow more than 300 m below its lip). Gentle ground keeps a share of what comes down to it (alluvial plains); a deep closed hollow keeps all (a basin); at the sea half is laid at the mouth (a delta) and half along the shelf beside it. Of 4.62 million km³ shed each million years, 46% rests on plains, 36% in deltas, 16% on shelves and 2% in closed basins; all of it is accounted for. Each resting place has a thickness (today's rates kept up for the 190 million years since the First Land broke up, no more than 12 km) and its kind; the greatest deltas, basins and plains are listed.
- *Causal tests* (`tests/eym-sediment.test.mjs`): steeper ground, more runoff or softer rock wears faster; nothing is lost on the way; twice the wear upstream, a bigger delta; a flat coastal plain keeps a share that a steep slope does not; a deep hollow keeps everything and a shallow dip drains through.
- *Standing checks:* every square's crust between the normal thickness and the limit; all that is shed comes to rest; the land's mean wear between 20 and 120 m a million years.
- *Not changed:* the maps. This layer is read by nothing drawn yet; Resources will read it (coal, oil and gas, sedimentary ores, salt and placers want the basins' and deltas' thickness and age; ore bodies want the unroofing).
- *Still to build:* the land itself reshaped by its wearing down over the plate history (the owner chose to leave that for later), and Caren's land carved to its drawn rivers (next).

## 0.39.0 (2026-11-21)

Ranges' heights by their histories (owner's direction of 20 November 2026, the causal-chain audit beyond rainfall, item 1, with items 2 and 3 in a reduced form). The far continents' ranges were a height for their kind plus some noise (collision 4,300 m and up to 1,500 more, accretion 2,500 and up to 1,100, old belts 1,700 and up to 900). Each now has a history, and its height follows from it (`scripts/eym-orogeny.mjs`, dials `orogeny`).

- *The model* (one history for each range). Where plates converge, a share of the convergence shortens and thickens the belt's crust (a subducting plate mostly goes down; a collision takes half). Thick crust floats high: about 150 m of height for each kilometre of excess crust (Airy isostasy). The land wears down, far faster the higher it stands, faster in wetter country and softer rock; as rock is taken off, the root rises, so 6.6 km of rock must go to lower the surface 1 km. Crust cannot thicken beyond about 70 km.
- *Set by Earth's ranges, not Eym's.* An Alpine collision (10 mm a year for 35 million years) comes out with summits near 4,250 m; a Himalayan one (45 mm a year) near 8,500 m; a coastal range over a plate going down at 65 mm a year near 4,800 m; a belt that stopped 300 million years ago keeps summits near 1,200 m.
- *What the plates' own motions give.* Each range's rate of convergence is the two plates' own, at its middle. Coastal ranges (27 mm a year for 210 million years): summits 3,260 to 3,500 m (they were 3,670 to 3,690). Today's collisions (12 to 18 mm a year): 3,920, 4,690 and 5,220 m, the faster the higher (they were 4,830 to 5,250). Belts of swept-up terranes (23 to 73 mm a year): 3,830 to 5,360 m (they were 2,810 to 3,130): Gaffa's fast southern margin now carries its highest range. The First Land's old seams, worn for 320 million years: 970 to 1,130 m, lowest on wet Gaffa (they were 2,300 to 2,500).
- *Recorded with each range:* its history (kind, rate, how long, when it stopped, the rain it wears under), its crust's thickness (38 to 53 km), its mean height, how deep it is unroofed (the old seams to their roots, 35 km: deep rocks at the surface, as the geology already says) and the rock it sheds each million years.
- *What was not known range by range is a dial, said plainly:* the old seams' closing (40 mm a year for 100 million years, ended 320 million years ago: the plate history's assembly), how long today's collisions have run (25 million years) and the sweeping-up of terranes (120 million years). The rain a range wears under is its latitude's on an all-ocean Eym, since the rain budget reads the heights.
- *Nothing else in the plate record changed:* the same seed was chosen; plates, poles, speeds, boundaries, continents, the ranges' courses, volcanoes, mantle and history are as they were. Caren's and Garyk's heights are not touched (they follow their maps and Caren's own natural history).
- *What follows.* Rain, rivers, country and everything downstream are made again from the new heights: 697 of Gaffa's 6,045 squares change kind; Gaffa's highest square rises by about 1,250 m.
- *Causal tests* (`tests/eym-orogeny.test.mjs`): faster or longer convergence, higher; a young collision under half the height of a grown one; a subduction range lower than a collision at the same speed; an old belt lower the longer ago it stopped, yet still hills after 600 million years; wetter country or softer rock, lower and more deeply unroofed; height is the crust's excess times the density contrast, and 6.6 km of rock goes for each kilometre the surface falls; no range above the crust's limit; and every shipped range's height is exactly what its recorded history gives.
- *Not yet built, said plainly:* a map of the land wearing down over the plate history (erosion square by square, with each rock's own rate), a planet-wide map of the crust's thickness, the sediment the ranges shed laid down in basins, shelves and deltas, and the drawn rivers with little water (0.37.0). Volcanoes' heights are still drawn from a range of values.
- *Tests changed, said plainly.* The two pins of the plate record and of the grown continents now leave out the ranges' heights and histories (which have tests of their own) and are recorded anew; everything else under them, the ranges' courses included, was checked the same as before. The fixed pin of the plate record's content is recorded anew with a note. The list of the plate generator's dial groups gains `orogeny`. No test is skipped or deleted.

## 0.37.0 (2026-11-20)

Rivers carry the climate (owner's direction of 20 November 2026; the second step of "fix the causal chain"). A river's water is the runoff of the land it truly drains, not a figure for its catchment's area or the width it is drawn at.

- *The rivers' rain is the budget's.* The monthly climate Caren's and Garyk's rivers run on (`eym-flow.json`) takes each square's twelve months of rain from the rain file (0.36.0); the sampled weather still gives the snow's share and the temperatures. Until now it was the daily weather's rain, which follows the kind of country and not the budget.
- *The drainage measures runoff.* As it routes water over the land (unchanged: the same 984 channels, point for point), it now also carries each node's own yearly runoff (rain less what soil and plant take, by its climate and height) downstream, and records for every channel point the mean runoff of the land it drains. A channel's valley is cut as wide as its water (the square root of its flow): wider in wet country, a thread where hardly anything runs off. The build makes the rivers' climate before the drainage, which reads it.
- *A river can dwindle and die.* Crossing ground that sheds no water of its own, a stream loses up to a fifth of what it carries for each 100 km to the air and its bed (dials `weather.rivers`). On Caren the land that sheds almost no water is the Sondel remnant's dry lee in the west (500 to 1,300 mm of rain with a long dry season, nearly all of it taken by soil and plant), and on Garyk its far north: streams there are threads, and water crossing them dwindles.
- *The drawn rivers' true catchments.* For each river drawn on the maps the drainage finds the land that truly drains to it and its runoff, at up to forty places along its course. Its flow, flood and low water (the river reports, fords, battle maps) now come from that, not from a catchment guessed from its drawn width. The drawn courses and widths stay as drawn.
- *Reported, not changed: drawn rivers with little water.* Of 42 drawn river courses, 12 carry less than a river's water in the model (under 10 m³/s in the mean, or under 100 m³/s where drawn two kilometres wide or more): the land's modelled heights send their water elsewhere, or their country is too dry. The standing checks list them (`rivers-flow`, a warning). Caren's greatest river carries about 3,400 m³/s. This is for the heights and erosion step, which must make the land explain the rivers the map draws.
- *The map against the rain, as a standing report* (`data/eym/caren.json`, `climate.mapAgainstRain`; a warning in the checks, neither side overridden): of Caren's 120 full squares, none of the 24 mostly forest has under 900 mm, 16 of the 50 mostly open have over 2,500 mm, and all 11 mostly tan have over 1,000 mm.
- *Causal tests* (`tests/eym-rivers-climate.test.mjs`): more rain in a catchment gives more than proportionally more runoff, and a dry land sheds none; twice the runoff upstream is twice the flow; a stream crossing desert loses a third of its water in 200 km; a bed is half as wide on a quarter of the runoff; the rivers' months are the rain file's.
- *What changes on the maps.* Valleys and river beds are a little wider in wet country and narrower in dry (about 57% of Caren's sampled Region tile pieces and 27% of Garyk's change, slightly). River reports change with the new flows: Garyk's delta river now floods for some days in most years, longest in the wettest.
- *Not in this step:* the far continents' rivers are still placed by the land drained alone, and the daily weather's own rain still follows the kind of country; both come next.
- *Tests changed, said plainly.* Three tests pinned to one place or year are re-pointed, because the climate there changed: the Garyk river's floods are now compared between the three wettest and the three driest of eleven years (they were pinned to years 55 and 56); the springs on Caren's limestone are looked for at 7.4° S 0.3° W (the old place, 3.4° S 0.8° W, is drier now and has none); the battle map of a stream is taken 20 km east of where it was (at the old place the stream no longer crosses the small map; the same was already true of other places before this change). No test is skipped or deleted.

## 0.36.0 (2026-11-20)

Rain by its causes, for the whole planet (owner's direction of 20 November 2026: "fix the causal chain"). Until now the land's yearly rain was a figure for its latitude times a few weak multipliers, so no sea, wind or mountain could make a desert near the equator. The rain is now a budget of the water the air carries (`scripts/eym-rain.mjs`, dials `rain`, written to `data/eym/rain.json` by `tools/generate-eym-rain.mjs`).

- *The budget.* For each place and month the air is followed back along the season's wind and forward again, with the water in the column above it: taken up over the sea toward what the sea's own temperature allows (6.4% less for each degree colder, while the column above stays as warm as its latitude's, so over cold water it is far from full); gathered under the rain belt and along the storm tracks and spread apart round them; rained out by how full the column is (the law measured over the tropical oceans, steep, with no floor); wrung out where broad high ground shortens the column or a narrow range lifts the low air, and dry behind it; given back by the land from soil and leaf (nearly all of it in dry country: Budyko's curve); thinned where a wind is squeezed through a gap and fans out beyond; and mixing all the while with the air round it. Low air climbs high ground only near its top; the rest is turned aside.
- *The rain belt differs by longitude.* It stands over the warmest water: off a cold tongue, toward a warm pool, and over heated land as before. Its rain is strong where the sea under it is warm and weak where it is cold.
- *The season's winds.* Trades blowing toward the belt, turned westerly where they have crossed the equator to reach it (a monsoon), westerlies beyond the dry belts: so a range's dry side is the side the season's wind leaves it on.
- *Fitted to one thing only:* the rain by latitude of an all-ocean Eym, which the budget gives back within a tenth. The sizes of the causes are from measured physics (named with each dial). No number is set for any place.
- *Causal tests* (`tests/eym-rain.test.mjs`, a new rule for every stage): colder sea offshore, drier coast (a sea 4 °C colder upwind takes three fifths of a coast's rain; 6 °C leaves a Peru); a range's lee is drier than its windward foot in every month, and the higher the drier (a 1,500 m range leaves a seventh, as Madagascar's does; 3,000 m a thirtieth); an equatorial basin behind a 3,000 m range gets about 120 mm, and a corridor between highlands a third of open country's rain; the belt leaves cold water and goes to warm; the wind turns with the season; land that gave nothing back would be drier; the islands' ridge is in the sea's temperatures and colder water there means less rain there.
- *The island chain, checked.* It is in the data: the sea over the ridge is 0.9 to 1.2 °C colder than its latitude's (0.5 °C before the islands), and at Caren's coast beside it 0.6 to 1.7 °C by season (0.5 °C before). On the equator itself the upwelling was already at the model's ceiling, so most of the cold tongue was there before the islands.
- *Caren, measured again* (`docs/eym-canon.md`, "As measured"). Found: the cold tongue, the cooler north, the cool plateau, south Caren's wet and dry seasons (Dree 1,716 mm with four dry months), snow only above 4,800 m. Not found: the sea's 5 to 8 °C swing (0.5 °C), the belt south of the equator for more of the year (4 months), a drier north (2,545 mm against 1,651 mm), a dry, frosty season on the plateau (no dry month), frost at 1,500 to 2,000 m, the rain shadows south-west of Mt. Weth and round Menera (Mt. Oplin's lee has a third less; Mt. Weth's none: a lone massif is flowed round, and the air reaches it from the south-east for much of the year). One result of 0.35.0 is reversed: the old rule's shadow south-west of Mt. Weth is not there.
- *The Molkath desert is still not reached, and nothing is tuned.* Measured now as the map's tan ground round the Sondel's remnant: its west side, the remnant's lee, is the driest (1,536 mm in the middle, five dry months; 1,228 mm with the wall standing), its east side the wettest (4,420 mm). A range across the wind east of it, or a far colder sea there, would dry it; the map shows neither. For the world's author to decide.
- *The planet's country follows.* Every land square's rain, driest month and kind of country are made again from the budget, and soils and suitability with them. The far continents' country now follows their mountains and coasts instead of bands of latitude: Gaffa 2,802 of 6,045 squares change kind, Khohalis 502 of 3,494, Demani 589 of 1,908, Garyk 89 of 201, Caren 52 of 175. On Caren the west turns to savanna (a long dry season in the remnant's and the plateau's lee) and the east stays forest and woodland. The map still rules what is drawn on Caren and Garyk: forest where it draws forest, tan ground dry grassland.
- *The map against the rain* (reported, neither overridden): one of Caren's mostly forest squares has under 900 mm, sixteen of its mostly open squares over 2,500 mm, all nine of its mostly tan squares over 1,000 mm.
- *A phone draws the largest regions of country,* up to a number for the zoom (as it does rivers and waters): a varied climate makes more small regions than a phone should draw.
- *Still to come in this block:* rivers that carry the climate (discharge from runoff), the daily weather's rain by the same budget, then heights, erosion and sediment by their causes.
- *Tests changed, said plainly.* The tests of the old Caren rain rule's causes (belt, shadow, lift, cold sea, channel, in `tests/eym-caren-climate.test.mjs`) are replaced by the causal tests of the planet's rule, which ask more; Caren's temperature rule keeps its own test. The pinned map of the canon's expectations has one line changed (the shadow south-west of Mt. Weth: found before, not found now). The Molkath test now measures the tan ground by side instead of one box. Dree's country may be savanna or open woodland (it was pinned as savanna). The survey's list of dial groups gains `rain` and `weather`. No test is skipped or deleted without a replacement.

## 0.34.0 (2026-11-20)

Caren's heights follow from its natural history (canon of 20 November 2026; `docs/eym-canon.md`, "Caren's natural history"). One rule (`scripts/eym-caren-relief.mjs`) gives the land its height at every scale, from the dials in `scripts/eym-world-inputs.mjs` (`caren`); a survey of the result (`data/eym/caren.json`) is in the build chain and the world's standing checks. Caren's outline, its waters, forests and names are unchanged; Garyk is unchanged.

- *The northern plateau.* North of the Sondel the interior is a plateau at 1,340 to 1,870 m (1,630 m in the middle), about 10.6 °C cooler than the coast: a dome raised by a swell of the mantle (a push of 960 m at most) under crust 35 to 41 km thick, with nothing left unsupported. Every northern coast rises to it by a long ramp (125 to 145 km at a mean of 1.0 to 1.4%; the steepest 10 km is 1.7%), and no land within 12 km of a northern coast stands above 124 m.
- *The great rivers* (the Myra, the Thran, the Palma) lie in valleys cut through the dome, tidal for their lowest 75 km and climbing gently above (73 m at the Myra's head, 310 km up). The Palma, which the tracing had taken for a lake, is a river. On Caren a wide river's real channel is now up to 3 km wide (it was at most 1.2 km), and each great river (the Drong, the Molkath, the Taunmis and the Sherval among them) widens to as much as 7 km over its tidal reach. Garyk's rivers are as they were.
- *The peaks come out lower, and none keeps snow.* A massif of hard rock stands at most about 2,900 m above the plateau round it (2,300 m as Earth's largest such massifs do, 300 m from small faults, 300 m from the crust's rebound). That gives Mt. Oplin 4,567 m, Mt. Shila 3,992 m, Mt. Danrich 3,582 m and Mt. Weth 3,437 m; the data had peaks of about 5,300 m. ## 0.25.0 (2026-11-18)

Stage 4 of Eym's physical history: rivers through the year. With it, the loose ends of Stage 3.

- The rivers' monthly climate (`tools/generate-eym-flow.mjs`, written to `data/planet-cartography/abydos/eventide-flow.json`, 18 KB): four years of the weather sampled every 5 hours over Caren, Garyk and their islands, kept month by month (rain, the share that fell as snow, and warmth). `npm run build` makes it again whenever its sources change.
- River flow (`scripts/eym-river-flow.mjs`): a water balance turns each month's rain and warmth into runoff. Snow lies while it freezes and melts as it warms (headwaters are cooled for their height, so high mountains feed a spring flood). The soil holds some 150 mm. What it cannot hold runs off at once or soaks down to groundwater, which keeps rivers running through the dry season. A big river smooths and delays its flood. Every river and drainage channel gets a flow for every day of the year, with a stage: dry, low, normal, high or in flood.
- Wet and dry years: as on Earth, the oscillation of the great equatorial ocean brings more rain to the eastern side of its basin in a warm year and less to the western side. On top of that is a smooth year-to-year swing of some ±15%. So some years flood and some are droughts. A Garyk river near its delta floods for about five weeks in an ordinary year and not at all in a dry one, and runs low for nearly half of every year.
- Rivers' beds follow the water they carry: Caren's and Garyk's rivers are now 0.7–1.35 times as wide as before, wider where more of the rain runs off (most of Caren) and narrower where less does (much of Garyk). The Region layer, local maps and battle maps show the new widths.
- Battle maps follow the river's stage (`scripts/eym-battle-rules.mjs`):
  - At low water a river's bed is partly bare gravel bars, and it can be waded: knee-deep at the edge of the water (difficult terrain), waist-deep further in (greater difficult), deeper in the middle (swimming).
  - Streams run dry (ordinary ground) or swell (greater difficult terrain).
  - In flood the river and its streams cannot be forded (Athletics DC 20), streams are torrents, and the low ground near them lies under knee-deep floodwater (greater difficult terrain).
  - The GM's note says which. The pictures draw the water at the stage, and the tier-2 pictures now have their own colours for the new ground and for the tide's flats and surf, which had fallen back to grass.
- The "Here and now" card has a River line: the nearest river or stream within 3 km, its stage and flow, its bed, and whether it can be waded at a ford. If it can't, it gives how many days until it can (the delay a crossing adds to a journey); a bed over 400 m wide needs a bridge or a ferry. The map server passes the river's stage through to battle maps, checked field by field.
- Springs: where porous rock (limestone, marble, sandstone, volcanic rock) holds groundwater, streams rise from springs and water seeps out at the foot of slopes, more of both the more rain soaks down. Each wets a small spring-fed fen on gentle ground. Springs are marked on the local map.

- A wind and wave climate (`tools/generate-eym-wind.mjs`, written to `data/planet-cartography/abydos/eventide-wind.json`, 37 KB): four years of the weather and the swell of Stages 2a–2b, sampled every 7 hours over Caren, Garyk and their islands. Off every coast it gives the wave energy and the sand-moving power of the wind arriving from each of 16 directions; over the land, the wind's sand-moving power by direction, the year's rain and how dry the driest season is; and a loess map. `npm run build` makes it again whenever its sources change.
- Coasts now take their wave energy from that climate, not from a fixed measure of how open they are (`scripts/eym-landforms.mjs`). Garyk's coasts facing the trade winds take the biggest seas (about 3.4 m, mostly from the north-east): their hard-rock headlands are cliffs, their beaches wide. Its west coast, in the lee, is calmer: more of it is beach and less is cliff. Caren's western bay stays calm salt marsh.
- Coastal dunes: behind a well-fed beach under strong onshore winds, a belt of dunes up to a couple of kilometres deep, bare sand at the front and grassed behind, with crests up to some 18 m. They are common on Garyk's trade-wind coasts and rare on Caren, whose equatorial winds are light.
- Inland (`scripts/eym-aeolian.mjs`): where the ground is dry, bare and windy, the wind heaps sand into dunes, scours stony desert pavement, and carves yardangs from soft rock. Eym is maritime and humid, so this is limited: nothing on Caren, and in Garyk's dry-season north a few fields of low, grassed dunes with sinuous crests. Nowhere on Caren or Garyk is arid enough for pavement or yardangs (the rules are there for drier places).
- Loess: dust lifted from dry, bare, windy ground with fine sediment is carried with the year's net wind and laid where grass and woods trap it, on lowland and gentle upland. Garyk's dry-season north has a blanket up to about 5 m deep. Two metres or more is the soil (`geologyAt` reports "deep, wind-laid loess (very fertile, easily worked)"), it smooths the land, and it raises how much is farmed on the Region layer and on local maps.
- The Region layer moves to model v4 (`eym-region-layer-v4`): its tiles have new paths in the tile store and are rendered again after this release.
- Caren's and Garyk's outlines are unchanged; the wind works inside them.
- Tests: a new `tests/eym-aeolian.test.mjs` (6 tests). Updated: `tests/eym-landforms.test.mjs` now expects the Region model `eym-region-layer-v4` (was v3), and `tests/release-package.test.mjs` expects 0.24.0.

## 0.22.0 (2026-11-18)

Stage 2c of Eym's physical history: tides from the two moons and the Sun, and tidal flats on battle maps.

- The tide-raising pull of Mitkear, the White Moon and the Red Moon (`scripts/eym-tides.mjs`), each from where it stands over Eym at the moment, from the same orbits as the night sky: the equilibrium tide in its twice-daily, daily and slow parts, reduced by the planet's elastic yielding. The White Moon pulls about 0.88 as hard as Earth's moon, the Red Moon and the Sun about half as hard. The White Moon's tide comes round every 12.4 hours and the Red Moon's every 13.4, so springs and neaps are uneven: some springs much bigger than others, some neaps almost still.
- The tide on each coast: the ocean answers about 2.2 times the equilibrium tide, and a wide, shallow continental shelf (measured from Eym's sea floor) amplifies and delays it. Folo, behind a 180 km shelf, has a mean range of about 2.2 m (0.5 m at neaps, up to 3.9 m at the greatest springs); Garyk's south-east port and open-ocean islands about 1 m; a few coasts off Caren and Gaffa 5–6 m at springs.
- The "Here and now" card shows the tide on the nearest coast: the height now, rising or falling, the next high and low water (universal time), springs or neaps, and the day's range.
- Coastal battle maps follow the tide by the hour: at low water the sea draws back over the shore, uncovering wet sand flats, tidal mud or weedy rock (difficult terrain; mud greater difficult); at high water the lower beach is shallow surf (difficult terrain); at half tide the map is as before. The waterline moves at a typical slope for the shore (sand 1 in 20, mud 1 in 250, rock 1 in 8). The brief gives the tide and when it turns, the painting prompt names the flats and surf, and the web app's map server passes the tide through.
- Tests: a new `tests/eym-tides.test.mjs` (6 tests); `tests/release-package.test.mjs` updated for 0.22.0.

## 0.21.0 (2026-11-18)

Stage 2b of Eym's physical history: waves, swell and sailing times.

## 0.20.0 (2026-11-17)

Stage 2a of Eym's physical history: ocean currents, sea temperature and climate.

- Wind-driven surface currents from the wind belts, Eym's rotation and the coastlines (`tools/generate-eym-climate.mjs`, solved for four seasons on a 2° grid), with Ekman drift and upwelling. A strong circumpolar current runs east in the southern westerlies; gyres and boundary currents form where Caren, Garyk and Gaffa block the flow. The currents are in their own file, `data/planet-cartography/abydos/eventide-ocean.json`, loaded only for the currents overlay; `scripts/eym-climate.mjs` reads them back (`currentAt`).
- A sea-temperature anomaly map by season (`data/eym/climate.json`), carried by the year-round currents: warm and cold wakes keep their sign all year, are at most about 2 °C, are strongest in each hemisphere's winter and fade 20–30° downstream. Each wide equatorial basin has a warm pool in the west and a cold tongue in the east.
- A slow El Niño-like oscillation, deterministic from the clock (three cycles of 3.7, 5.3 and 2.6 years), warming or cooling the east of the great equatorial ocean by up to about 2 °C (`ensoIndex`, `describeEnso`).
- Fed into what exists: coastal and sea temperatures (`seasonalTemperature` takes the anomaly), rain on nearby coasts (wetter beside warm water, drier beside cold), biomes, the weather and battle-map conditions.
- The tropical rain belt follows the Sun through the year, at 0.4 of its declination (weaker than Earth's, as on an aquaplanet). Daily ranges on land are narrowed to 2–4 °C and the inland seasonal swing is weighed down.
- Tropical cyclones form only over sea at 25.5 °C or warmer, so they come from the warm pools: about 58 a year (threshold and rate changed). Rain was recalibrated with `tools/calibrate-eym-weather.mjs`, so mean rainfall matches the climate as before.
- The weather on any given date changes. On the land: 3 of Caren's 128 cells change biome (rainforest and seasonal forest), 1 of Garyk's 109 (steppe to savanna), 18 elsewhere. Caren's and Garyk's stored region tiles are left as they are until Stage 3.
- Tests: a new `tests/eym-climate.test.mjs` (6 tests). Updated: the pinned hash of `docs/system-orbital-distances.csv` in `tests/cartography-acceptance.test.mjs`, because the cartography fingerprint now covers the climate.
